OpenVEF v0.6 Audit: Charter and Protocol
Nov 22, 2026 · @Conductors
1. Verdict: PASS WITH CORRECTIONS (not freezable)
v0.6 moves in the right direction:
• intention-to-treat (ITT) validity;
• full-failure denominators;
• an evaluator-only mask;
• a narrowed prompt factor;
• a common-trace panel;
• an honest prior-art boundary.
The direction is sound, but several definitions are still wrong or under-specified in ways that would bias the reported diagnostics or make the treatment unverifiable. v0.6 must not be frozen, implemented, or used to start confirmatory LLM generation until the blockers below are fixed and the owner has decided each proposal.

2. Denominator and metric audit
2.1 Does the RQ separate the four layers?
Only partly.
• The Protocol body does define the four layers: ITT validity (§13.1), joint exposure and detection (§13.3), conditional E_i/S_i (§13.2), and descriptive M_i.
• The RQ text does not map onto them. Part (b) "driver exposure" and part (c) "detection of exposed behaviour by each artifact's own checker" read as the conditional quantities E_i and S_i. The confirmatory H2 tests the joint ITT rates J_exposure and J_detection.
• The joint rates are cumulative products of the stages, approximately J_detection ≈ V × E × S (see the identity in 2.4). A prompt effect on J_detection can therefore come entirely from validity. The separation of stages lives only in the conditional diagnostics, which are deliberately not confirmatory.
• The design is coherent; the RQ wording is not.
Proposed RQ wording:
…how do two prompt-organization formats carrying identical semantic information affect (a) artifact validity per scheduled call, (b) joint masked exposure and (c) joint own-checker detection per scheduled call × primary mutant? Conditional own-driver exposure and own-checker detection among valid artifacts, and miss composition, are reported as labelled diagnostics.
Also: make H1 estimation-only (rates and CIs per arm). The test of the validity difference is H2(a). Holm then runs over exactly three tests. Currently H1 says "estimate and compare" and H2(a) compares again.

Three consequences:
1.  (B1) E_i depends on the checker. Four classes with complete traces and known non-exposure or exposure drop out of E_i purely because of what the checker did. Example: a checker that is stricter than the mask and fails on many raw-only divergences removes those unexposed pairs from E_i's denominator, which raises the measured "driver" exposure.
2.  (B2) S_i hides checker crashes. A checker that crashes on exposed traces loses those pairs from both numerator and denominator. Its S_i is computed only over traces it survived.
3.  E_i and J_exposure disagree on what counts as exposure. J_exposure counts exposure recorded before an error, a timeout or a checker crash; E_i counts none of them. So J_exposure ≠ V × the mean of E_i, and readers will try to reconcile them.
2.3 Corrected definitions (recommended)
For valid artifact i, let T_i be the complete-trace, valid pairs: all normal outcomes, both anomaly classes, and both checker-error classes. Exposure is a function of the trace alone, so:
• X_i = pairs in T_i with a masked divergence = DET + PND + CHECKER_ERROR_AFTER.
• E_i = X_i / |T_i|. This is checker-independent by construction.
• S_i = DET_i / X_i. Checker crash on an exposed trace counts as non-detection (primary). As a sensitivity, count a crash as detection.
• M_i = PND_i / (NP_i + PND_i). Keep it descriptive. The identity M = E(1−S)/(1−ES) holds exactly only on the normal-outcome subset C_i. State that, and compute the identity check on C_i-restricted quantities only.
• Truncated pairs (driver/simulator error, timeout) are excluded from T_i. Report them per artifact. As a one-sided sensitivity, add "exposed before termination" pairs to X_i and all truncated pairs to the denominator. That gives a lower bound on exposure, because non-exposed truncated pairs might have exposed later.
• Invalid pairs are excluded from T_i and count as zero in ITT rates. This is consistent with v0.6, but the Charter's §12 sentence ("invalid pairs are separately excluded from the eligible-pair denominator") must say excluded from conditional denominators, retained as zero in ITT denominators.
Then define ITT exposure consistently: J_exposure = Σ_valid X_i / (|G_cd| · |M_d|). Report truncated-before-termination exposure as a separate ITT sensitivity, not inside the primary numerator, so that J_exposure and E_i use the same exposure rule.
2.4 Identities that should hold, and be unit-tested
With the corrected definitions, per design and arm:
• J_exposure = V_cd · (Σ_valid X_i / Σ_valid |M_d|).
• J_detection = V_cd · (Σ_valid DET_i / Σ_valid |M_d|).
• Σ over all pair classes = |valid artifacts| · |M_d|.
Add these as F15 assertions. The current F15 phrase "sums to eligible/scheduled totals as defined" is not testable.

2.6 Other metric issues
• Holm unit. H2 says "within-design artifact-level permutation". For ITT outcomes the exchangeable unit is the scheduled slot, including invalid slots. Write "slot-level".
• Checker determinism for validity. Validity runs the checker once on the null trace, so a non-deterministic checker can pass once and later produce ANOMALY_PIPELINE. Run the checker on the null trace at least twice as part of validity. This costs almost nothing.
• Does a checker run on truncated traces? Not stated. State that checkers are run only on complete traces, so that CHECKER_ERROR_* is well defined.
3. Mask withholding and the prompt factor
3.1 Is withholding the mask coherent?
Yes, with two conditions that v0.6 does not yet state.
The mask is "derived from the frozen specification" (P §9). Every don't-care window must therefore already be stated in the specification authors receive. If it were not, an author could not know, for example, that FIFO data is unconstrained while valid = 0.
In that case a correct-looking checker that compares data in that window would hit one of two failures:
• Null rejection: it rejects the null trace if its model disagrees with the RTL's arbitrary value, which makes the artifact invalid.
• ANOMALY_CHECKER_STRICTER_THAN_MASK: it agrees with the RTL by chance, then fails on mutants that alter don't-care data.
Either way, the outcome would be decided by information the evaluator holds and the author lacks.
So withholding the mask is coherent only if:
1.  Every mask entry is traceable to a specification clause. Add a mask-to-specification traceability table to the evaluator pack, signed by the mask reviewer. Any mask entry with no clause is a defect in the specification, not a mask choice. Make this an F18 check.
2.  The claimed benefit is stated honestly. Withholding removes an explicit, machine-readable checklist, not information. A diligent author can reconstruct the mask from the specification. P §3 says it "avoids telling generated checkers exactly which outputs/cycles will be scored". That is true of the file, but not of the information. Reword it to say withholding removes the explicit scoring list while the obligations remain in the specification.
The "reference model" wording contradicts the information boundary.
• Charter §8: "The reference model/API may supply enough specification semantics for a checker to derive expected values".
• Charter §9 puts a "reference implementation" in the evaluator pack.
• The Protocol's authoring list (P §3) contains no reference model.
If authors received an executable reference model, a checker could call it directly. The checker task would collapse, and the reference model would implicitly encode the comparison boundary. Decide: no reference model or API in the authoring pack. Authors write their own expected-value logic from the specification. Delete the Charter §8 sentence or reword it accordingly.
The checker contract (P §10) lets expected values depend on "prior public-output/history fields". That is acceptable, since legitimate scoreboards track DUT-reported state. Note the consequence, though: a checker that derives expectations from the DUT's own (possibly faulty) outputs will accept self-consistent corruption. That is a real checker weakness the study should measure, not a contract violation. State it so it is not later treated as an anomaly.

4. Common-trace panel and instrument validation (F16–F18)
4.1 The panel is feasible: compute is trivial, but it is not yet well defined
Every checker is a pure function over trace files. Running about 20 checkers over one or two streams × (null + about 100 mutants) is minutes of CPU. The problems are definitional.
(a) Compatibility conflates three different outcomes. The Charter says checkers that "cannot process" a shared null trace are incompatible. The decision register (doc 16 §5) says a checker that fails a valid null trace is "incompatible/false-alarm". These are different events. Define a per-stream partition for each valid checker:
• PANEL_ERROR: crash, schema mismatch or unsupported field. Compatibility failure.
• PANEL_FALSE_ALARM: returns FAIL on a null trace. This is an error of commission and must be reported as a false-alarm rate, not hidden inside "incompatible".
• PANEL_COMPATIBLE: returns PASS on every null trace of that stream.
Score sensitivity only for compatible checkers, per stream. A checker that fails a stream's null trace would otherwise appear to "detect" every mutant on that stream.
(b) The corpus must be legal and generated through the same harness.
• Legality. A raw seeded random stream can violate the specification's legal-input assumptions (for example, writing while full where that is undefined). Spec-correct checkers may then reject null traces for legitimate reasons, and that would be counted as false alarms. Generate the random corpus with a reactive, legality-respecting random driver under the same harness, alignment and paired-run rules.
• Invalid pairs. Classify panel pairs with the same invalid-pair rule.
• Directed suite. Its authors must write the directed corner-case suite against the specification only, before generation and independently of any LLM output (already required), and must satisfy the same legality constraints.
(c) A single null trace per stream gives the false-alarm rate almost no resolution. Each checker sees one null trace per stream, so its false-alarm estimate is 0 or 1. Use several benign traces per stream: k seeds of the random driver on the null netlist, plus RTL-versus-null pairs. Freeze k.
(d) Exposure conditioning is correct but must be fixed explicitly. Sensitivity should be computed over the same set of (stream, mutant) cases with a masked divergence on the shared trace, identical for every checker. That is the point of standardisation. Also report the panel analogue of the stricter-than-mask anomaly: FAIL on cases with raw-only divergence.
(e) The prompt-arm estimand is missing. P §13.4 lists what is reported but not the comparison. The panel's main value is the arm difference in standardised sensitivity, and in false-alarm and compatibility rates. Pre-register it as secondary. Report it two ways: ITT over all scheduled slots, with an invalid or incompatible checker counting as zero sensitivity, and conditionally over compatible checkers. Compatibility can differ by arm, which would be another post-treatment selection.
(f) Clustering. The mutant set is a fixed, declared population, not a sample. State that panel inference is conditional on the declared mutant population, so resampling at the artifact (checker) level is sufficient and a two-way bootstrap is unnecessary. P §14 leaves the "hierarchical procedure" as TO FREEZE; this resolves it. Treat streams as fixed strata.
(g) Limit. Panel sensitivity measures checkers on the panel's exposure profile, not on what LLM drivers typically expose. Say so, and do not present it as "true checker quality" (P §13.4 already partly does).
