OpenVEF Step 2 Audit: Functional Qualification and Mutation Analysis
Nov 22, 2026 · @Conductors
1. Overall verdict: PASS WITH CORRECTIONS
The note's central position is right and well hedged. Functional qualification, activation/propagation/detection (A/P/D) and stimulus-versus-checker diagnosis are prior art, and OpenVEF does not invent them. Most bibliographic facts check out. Six corrections are needed:
1.  The 2014 IEEE Transactions on Computers (TC) paper is not functional qualification in the note's own sense. It defines a mutant as killed when the outputs differ from the fault-free run (Sec. 3.2 of the university-repository manuscript). It does not require a testbench failure. The note groups it with work that requires a failing test.
2.  The literature uses two different meanings of "propagation". The DATE 2009 paper, the Certess brief and Große et al. say propagation to an observable point such as the outputs. A Certitude user paper (DVCon) classifies a fault as non-propagated when it does not reach a checker. Under the second meaning, a fault that reaches an output no checker examines is non-propagated in Certitude terms but PND in OpenVEF. The note's mapping ("NP merges non-activation and non-propagation") holds only under the first meaning, and it also leaves out raw divergences that fall outside the mask.
3.  The note understates Huang et al. (2015). Its checker "completeness" term is killed mutants divided by mutants whose coverage fluctuated. That is a checker-side share among exposed mutants, a direct precedent for OpenVEF's secondary checker_gap. Its "alive + fluctuated = checker insufficient" row is the analogue of PND.
4.  Stuck-at fault grading is never discussed. The audit brief asks whether it is distinguished from design-bug mutation. OpenVEF's Yosys/MCY netlist signal mutants (inversions and constant drives on netlist signals) are structurally closer to manufacturing fault models than to design-bug mutation. That needs saying.
5.  Three Huang numbers could not be located. The final values 0.91 (random), 0.92 (directed), and the CPU's 0.48 → 0.91 over ten iterations were not found in the text I could access (Section 3 explains what I accessed). Verify them in Tables 2–4 before citing them.
6.  The DATE 2009 paper has vendor co-authors. Two of its five authors were Certess employees, and its experiments use Certitude. It is peer-reviewed, but it is not independent of the vendor. The note treats it as purely academic.
What I read. I read the extracted text of three sources: DATE 2009 (full proceedings PDF), the TC 2014 accepted manuscript, and Huang et al. (journal HTML, with equations and table cells not rendered). For the rest I had abstracts, metadata, first-page excerpts or search-index excerpts only. Section 3 gives the exact access level per source, and confidence is lowered wherever access was partial. No visual page images were inspected for any source.

5. OpenVEF-specific implications
Nothing here changes the research question, hypotheses, primary metric or statistics. The changes are definitional, a few reporting additions, and one extra validation check.
Hidden mismatches between the legacy literature and OpenVEF
These are my inferences from the sources above, not statements made by them.
1.  Suite verdict versus artifact verdict. In FQ, a fault is detected if any test in the suite fails. Non-activated means no test activates it. OpenVEF classifies each artifact–mutant pair separately. Legacy percentages are suite-level and cannot be compared with per-artifact shares.
2.  Fault dropping. Commercial FQ stops simulating a fault once one test detects it, and orders tests to do so sooner (DVCon practitioner paper). Per-test outcomes are therefore not recorded, so legacy results cannot be re-analysed at the test level.
3.  The propagation boundary depends on the checker in Certitude but not in OpenVEF. In OpenVEF it is a frozen specification mask. A missing check on a top-level output is non-propagated in Certitude terms but PND in OpenVEF. OpenVEF's checker share will therefore read higher than a Certitude-style "non-detected" share for the same testbench. This is a definitional effect, not a finding.
4.  Exposure proxy. Huang et al. use coverage-result fluctuation as the exposure condition. OpenVEF uses masked output divergence. The two structures are analogous, but they do not measure the same thing.
5.  Fault model. The FQ literature mutates RTL or SystemC source with design-error operators. OpenVEF mutates synthesised netlist signals. Its exposure step is fault simulation, and only the checker step is qualification.
6.  Online versus offline verdicts. Legacy testbenches fail online (assertions, scoreboards, $error). OpenVEF's offline checker over a captured trace is cleaner for attribution, but a checker that would have caught the bug online through a check the trace format does not support is scored as PND. The trace contract (all top-level ports, every cycle) makes this small for top-level-only checkers. It is still worth one sentence among the threats.

6. Revised prior-art boundary paragraph
Proposed replacement for the note's §8 and the Charter's prior-art statement:
Decomposing fault detection into activation, propagation and detection is established in both software testing (PIE; RIP/RIPR, including oracle revealability) and hardware verification. Observability-based coverage (Devadas et al., ICCAD 1996; OCCOM, DAC 1998 / TCAD 2001) addressed propagation to outputs. Functional qualification (Certitude, launched 2007; Bombieri et al., DATE 2009) counts a fault as detected only when the verification environment fails. Academic testbench-qualification work has separately assessed stimulus and checker sufficiency: Huang et al. (VLSI Design 2015), whose checker-completeness term is a checker-side share among exposed mutants, and coverage discounting (Lisherness and Cheng). OpenVEF therefore claims none of these ideas, and does not claim stimulus-versus-checker attribution itself; Verify the Verifier (2026) applies a comparable split to human-written UVM regressions. OpenVEF's proposed contribution is an empirical measurement under a specific design: newly LLM-generated driver/checker artifacts as the unit of analysis, a controlled comparison of prompt structure with matched information, propagation judged against a checker-independent specification mask, and Yosys/MCY netlist signal mutants (a structural fault population) evaluated by paired execution against a null netlist. Each element has precedents. Whether their combination is distinct is still open pending the LLM-era review, which must include mutation-based evaluations of LLM-generated testbenches such as AutoBench, GateTruth and SecTB-RTL. Results are defined relative to this fault population and mask, and are not comparable numerically with suite-level functional-qualification scores.
